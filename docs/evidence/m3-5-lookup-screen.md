## Procedure fixed before each sample's judgments

For each successfully downloaded corpus, enumerate zero-based JSON-array indices in file order, run `random.Random(42).shuffle(indices)`, save the **complete order**, then save its first 60 full original records. Route judgment uses only the projected user text; one ToolACE record was first inspected in full to understand its schema. No function name, answer or upstream task category was used as an Orchestra label.

After seeing the low broad ToolACE yield, an explicitly **adaptive discovery follow-up** was defined: first user text matches case-insensitive regex `\b(database|catalog|table|record|records|inventory|ID|IDs)\b`, excluding text starting `Role definition` or `角色定义`. This yields 2,213 records. Its complete seed-42 shuffle and first 60 records were saved **before judging that follow-up**. This biased sample is reported separately; it is not an independent random estimate for all ToolACE. It overlaps the broad sample by one excluded row. There are 179 distinct inspected source records across all three samples.

Every sampled row has its index, rank, exact query, verdict, reason, candidate route, request type and calculation verdict in ignored JSONL. `needs_review` includes source/context ambiguity and mixed requests; it is not counted as eligible. `provisionally_eligible` is a screen outcome, never `accepted`.

## What the inspected text establishes

ToolACE's useful candidates explicitly request records by IDs, rather than merely mentioning a stable factual topic. The follow-up includes a named HRDB Employee-table constraint, database email membership, car trim specifications, session/device/manager records, sports event records, a quote ID and painting conservation record. Historical tournament/season IDs supply the broad sample's lookup candidate. These retain their original wording. Bare Paris/exchange information and historical policy facts stay on the direct-answer boundary. Current prices, account status, trending items, live scores, external searches, execution/writes and unsupported image/audio operations do not become lookup merely because a tool dataset contains them.

Some ID-only candidates do not identify a named provider. They are retained **provisionally** under the explicit-record-ID interpretation; if human review requires a unique source beyond the record kind and ID, their yield falls. No source names were added to repair them. Multi-action requests with independent first actions are unresolved. Sports/current-service boundaries also require careful review; historical-looking IDs alone do not establish a frozen underlying record.

The SQL sample repeatedly asks about column values while naming no table/database in the question itself. The withheld CREATE statement is the missing grounding. Numbers in its questions are selection conditions, not sufficient numeric operands for calculation. The three direct candidates are stable named-entity facts; none establishes lookup capacity. The ToolACE sample also contains numeric material inside embedded multi-turn prompts, incomplete numerical requests and scheduling/execution tasks: **zero calculation candidates were established here**. This screen therefore does not relieve a calculation request-type bottleneck.

## Capacity and grouping consequences

M3 has 100 lookup rows. Reaching 625 or 1,000 per class requires another **525 or 900** lookup rows; this screen establishes only **18 unaccepted distinct candidates**, before grouping/review/parent checks. Independent eligible group capacity remains **unverified**, with zero newly accepted groups. ToolACE remains worth a bounded follow-up; SQL-create-context should not be bulk-collected for lookup under this adapter. xLAM remains access-blocked, not rejected for semantic quality.

All normal limits apply cumulatively: at most four rows per shared source/scenario/paraphrase group; no request type above 40% of a class; at least two sources per class and no source above 75%. In the enriched lookup sample, 15/17 candidates are record-details requests, one is membership and one constraint/filtering. Thus even a much larger ToolACE pool could hit the request-type cap well before the preferred target. A 625-row lookup class permits at most 250 rows of one request type; a 1,000-row class permits 400. These are ceilings, not available supply. Shared sports providers, reused fictional identifiers and near-identical formulations may further merge groups.

No parent-row or cross-corpus collision check was performed; no sealed data were opened for that purpose. No independence claim follows from distinct upstream array indices. Accepted quotas and feasible release size must wait for grouping, parent collision evidence, source mix, human decisions and blind review. Human review time was **not measured**: AI screening speed cannot estimate Altan's workload. Time a real small human batch before promising review throughput.

## Adaptive Glaive follow-up after the preceding screen

At the lead's request, a third accessible corpus was downloaded and screened after the results above. [Glaive's official dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/glaiveai/glaive-function-calling-v2/tree/e7f4b6456019f5d8bcb991ef0dd67d8ff23221ac) is pinned to `e7f4b6456019f5d8bcb991ef0dd67d8ff23221ac`; its README declares Apache-2.0. Downloaded JSON contains **112,960 records**, 271,190,065 bytes, SHA-256 `e9b5d671812b5ca2fbd7b625a37d5c99a19576c37252cdc806defe256aea6dad`. Each record has separate `system` and `chat` strings. The v2 card provides no detailed generation account. The publisher's [related v1 model card](https://huggingface.co/glaiveai/glaive-function-calling-v1) explicitly describes Glaive-platform synthetic training data. Treat this as a **public synthetic-family source with incomplete v2 generation provenance**, not a verified human-authored collection. Exact v2 generators and human contribution remain unverified; retain Apache attribution/license conditions if later adopted.

The adapter requires `chat` to start with `USER: ` and takes the exact substring before the first newline-delimited `ASSISTANT:`. It removes only role/separator markup, adds no instruction and appends no function metadata. All first-turn numerical inputs must already be present; later user replies are withheld. Examples asking to **write** SQL, a formula or code remain direct-answer candidates even when they mention a named table. Upstream function-call answers are withheld.

A parser correction matters to the audit. The initial implementation required three newlines before `ASSISTANT:`; this incorrectly treated 33,992 otherwise valid records as parse failures. Initial complete orders and samples were saved before text inspection. Inspecting the delimiter variation led to a mechanical correction to one-or-more newlines, **before formal verdict files were written**; the corrected parser extracts all 112,960 first turns. Initial artifacts were retained with `-initial` in their filenames, rather than overwritten. This is an adaptive, disclosed parser correction, not a prospectively untouched experiment.

For the corrected screen, save complete `random.Random(42)` shuffled orders and then sample **30 broad**, **60 structured-keyword**, and **60 math-keyword** records before judgments. The structured filter is the same word-boundary expression as ToolACE; its corrected pool has **5,904** rows. The math filter is `\b(calculate|compute|convert|sum|average|median|mean|percent|percentage|interest|area|volume|distance|factorial|square root|multiply|divide|subtract|add|total)\b`, case insensitive; its corrected pool has **24,645** rows. These are descriptive discovery filters, not labels. All three corrected samples happen to have no upstream-index overlap: **150 distinct records**, deduplicated in the audit.

The initial valid three-newline **structured subgroup** had 456 matches and a saved sample of 60. Those valid first turns were also formally screened as a separate, biased exploratory subgroup after the correction; none overlaps the corrected 150. The initial broad/math outputs are retained as superseded parser diagnostics with a `not_screened_superseded_parser_diagnostic` reason per row and do not enter reported candidate totals. The combined formally screened Glaive set therefore has **210 distinct upstream records**. Do not combine its subgroup yields as a corpus-wide random rate.
