# Follow-up research on Jev routing errors

November 23, 2026. Three checks of the archived predictions sharpen the explanation of Jev’s mistakes and show what a follow-up study could measure. The checks were chosen after the test review. Jev’s original test macro-F1 is 0.964. [Frozen test record](m4-final-test.md) and [report evidence map](../../report/README.md#supervisor-follow-up-november-19-2026)

## What the archived decisions show

**The amount separates the observed conversion errors.** The test set has 13 CLINC150 calculation requests of type `per-unit-scaling`, spread over nine groups. All four conversions asking about one unit and both asking about half a unit went to direct answer. All seven with a larger amount went to calculation. In group g449, Jev gave direct answer 0.99 for teaspoons in one tablespoon (q0605) and calculation 0.73 for tablespoons in five teaspoons (q0632). The pair shares the units, but the direction and phrasing changed too. Six of the seven larger amounts used digits. Jev also routed the remaining request, with five pounds spelled out, to calculation. Five of the six misses concern kitchen measures, and the misses span only three groups. One possible explanation is that familiar unit equivalences sound like facts to recall. The 17 validation items under the same source, type and label contain no one-unit or half-unit request, so they provide no independent check of that pattern. [Frozen prompt](../../src/orchestra/jev.py), [release](m3-5-dataset.md) and [test review](m4-final-test.md#test-mistakes-interpreted-after-the-freeze)

**The archive has no irrelevant passage attachments in the expansion test set.** Of the 549 expansion test queries, 180 had a recorded passage. All 180 required retrieval. All 44 with a recorded table required lookup. The other 325 had neither attachment kind. This follows the collection design and matters for a proposed metadata experiment. A router could predict the labels of all 224 attachment-bearing queries from type alone. The saved predictions came from a text-only router. A new evaluation would need, for example, a request to summarize an attached article and a request to rewrite a pasted sentence while the same article is attached. Only the first requires opening it. [Collection rules](../collection-brief-m3-5.md) and [release and attachment record](m3-5-dataset.md)

A passage can be missing from the input even when its reference is visible. Of the 180 test requests with a recorded passage, 26 contained the whole word “below”. Jev routed 22 to retrieval and four to direct answer (q2361, q2670, q2724, q2818). The corresponding validation counts were 27 correct out of 28. This retrospective text search gives examples for a controlled rewrite. It cannot tell whether Jev attended to that particular word. [Report cue analysis](../../report/README.md) and [frozen predictions](m4-final-test.md)

## Probe which changes should move the decision

Gardner et al. used small, human-authored edits that typically change the accepted answer. They scored *contrast consistency*, the proportion of sets on which every prediction was right, including the original. For example, the reported BERT accuracy on IMDb was 93.8% on original examples, 84.2% on edited examples, and 77.8% of sets were entirely correct. These values concern sentiment, while the design transfers to routing. [Gardner et al., Findings of EMNLP 2020, §2.2 and Table 2](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.117/)

For Jev, write fresh pairs and agree on their labels before a run. Moving a passage from inline text to a separate file that must be opened should change the route from direct answer to retrieval. Converting one tablespoon or five tablespoons to teaspoons should keep the route at calculation. With units and direction fixed, write the amounts as words and then as digits to disentangle the size of the amount from the way it appears on the page. Score both members of each pair and count how often a wording change alone flips the route. [Orchestra prediction contract](../data-and-evaluation.md#prediction-contract) and [contrast-set design](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.117/)

## Measure both sides of tool necessity

Ross et al. designed When2Call with synthetic cases where a tool call, a clarification question or a statement that the assistant cannot answer is the intended response. They scored free-form responses with a GPT-4-Turbo judge for closed models. GPT-4o achieved 61.3% behavior accuracy and called a tool on 26% of cases with no tool provided in the 300-case judged subset. The paper treats a direct answer as incorrect by construction and filters out mathematical calculations. Its discussion reports that adding only negative tool examples to the training blend of their 4B model improved When2Call scores while lowering accuracy on the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (BFCL) check of function names and arguments. This illustrates the trade-off between suppressing unnecessary calls and preserving necessary calls for that training setup. [Ross et al., NAACL 2025, Table 2, §4 and Table 4, §5 and Limitations](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.174/)

For Orchestra, a policy that asks for clarification or avoids retrieval more often could reduce some errors while introducing missed retrievals. Score *missed retrieval* among requests whose needed source must be retrieved and *unnecessary retrieval* among requests whose accepted first action differs. For a clarification policy, also report the question rate on already routable requests, separately from cases where an answer genuinely depends on resolving the referenced source. These are proposed outputs for new, pre-labelled groups. [When2Call, §5](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.174/) and [Orchestra’s report follow-up](../../report/README.md#supervisor-follow-up-november-19-2026)

## Related choices can make selection harder

Rabinovich and Anaby-Tavor expanded the set of available tools for 200 BFCL cases by adding roughly three semantically related functions per case. With the original request text held fixed, accuracy on the check of function names and arguments fell from 92.5% to 87.0% for GPT-4o-mini and from 91.5% to 89.0% for Claude 3.5 Sonnet. Wrong function selection and wrong arguments both contributed to those failures. Their rephrasing condition also showed declines, but their error analysis attributed most such failures to mismatches in parameter values, including alternative spellings or spacing for valid locations. The results concern tool calls with arguments, while Orchestra scores the first action only. [Rabinovich and Anaby-Tavor, TrustNLP 2025, §3.2 and Table 2](https://aclanthology.org/2025.trustnlp-main.20/)

Orchestra has four fixed action choices. The closest follow-up would keep those choices and input queries fixed while comparing the current label descriptions with descriptions that spell out the decision between document retrieval and knowledge lookup. For example, distinguish finding an answer in a referenced attachment from requesting a record from a structured catalog. Include both boundary directions and unrelated controls to see whether any change spreads errors to the other routes. This is a prompt hypothesis inspired by the tool-choice study. [Rabinovich and Anaby-Tavor, §3.2](https://aclanthology.org/2025.trustnlp-main.20/) and [the frozen Jev prompt](../../src/orchestra/jev.py)
